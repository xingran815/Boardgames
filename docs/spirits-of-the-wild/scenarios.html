Your very first turn

Easy

You are the start player. Setup is done: the bowl holds four regular stones, your board is empty, and the Coyote is still beside the table.

BowlBBRY
Spirit Stones out of the bag0 / 5
Your action cards
Take Oneface-upTake Oneface-upTake Twoface-downAdd Two & Take Oneface-upAdd Three & Move Coyoteface-upSpirit Power & Recoverface-up

Your question

a) Which of your action cards can you legally play right now?

b) You decide to take one blue stone. How many points is it worth right now on each animal, and where would you put it?

  1. All six cards
  2. Take One (either copy), Add Two & Take One, Add Three & Move Coyote
  3. Everything except Use Spirit Power & Recover Cards
  4. Only the two Take One cards
Show the answer

a) B. Four cards. As start player you flipped Take Two Stones face-down during setup, and Use Spirit Power & Recover Cards needs at least three of your other cards face-down (you have one). Add Three is legal, but with an empty opponent board the Coyote has nothing worth blocking.

b) Points for one blue stone right now:

Blue goes on…Points now
Rabbit (1 of 3 colours, incomplete)0
Turtle (1 stone)1
Salmon (1 colour)1
Owl (half a pair)0
Beaver (1 of a set)0

The first stone is worth at most 1 point anywhere, so think about the next stone instead. A second blue would lift a blue Turtle from 1 to 3 (+2) but would complete an Owl pair for +4. There are two blues in the bowl, so a good plan is Take One: blue → Owl (or Turtle). Your opponent may grab the other blue, but if it is still there next turn you can finish the pair.

Rule it teaches: Your first turn is only about legal cards and a sensible start; points come from finished sets. Manual §4 Setup step 6, §6 Use Spirit Power, §7 The five animals

Count the score

Easy

The game has just ended. Here is Mia's board. Count it before you open the answer.

Your question

How many points does Mia score in total (Owl pair = 4 points)?

  1. 31
  2. 38
  3. 40
  4. 46
Show the answer

C — 40 points.

AnimalPoints
Rabbit: red, blue — only 2 of 3, incomplete0
Turtle: 4 green = 9, Spirit Stone doubles it9 × 2 = 18
Salmon: 5 different colours9
Owl: blue pair + red pair (yellow single is worth 0)2 × 4 = 8
Beaver: purple three-of-a-kind; white pair unfinished5
Total40

Traps: 46 counts the unfinished Rabbit (+6); 31 forgets to double the Turtle. The lone white stone is not a Beaver pair, so there is no 2 and no 7-point bonus. 38 is the answer with the English 2017 Owl value of 3 per pair, not the 4 this manual uses.

Rule it teaches: Rabbit, Owl pairs and Beaver sets score only when complete; a Spirit Stone doubles one animal. Manual §7 The five animals, §11 Scoring

Turtle or Salmon?

Medium

Mid-game. Your Rabbit and Beaver are complete, and your opponent has put the Coyote on your Owl. Take One and Take Two are both face-up.

BowlYYBB

Your question

Which play gains the most points this turn?

  1. Take One: yellow → Salmon
  2. Take Two: both yellows → Turtle
  3. Take Two: one yellow → Turtle, one yellow → Salmon
  4. Take Two: yellow → Turtle and a blue for later
Show the answer

B — +8 points. The engine's best two-stone play is both yellows on the Turtle.

PlayGain
A: yellow → Salmon (9 → 13)+4
B: Turtle 3 → 5 yellow (6 → 14)+8
C: Turtle 6 → 9 and Salmon 9 → 13+3 +4 = +7
D: Turtle 6 → 9; the blue fits nowhere+3

One stone is worth more on the Salmon (+4 vs +3), but two stones are worth more together on the Turtle, because the 5th Turtle stone jumps from 9 to 14. Choice D is a trap: blue can't go on the full Rabbit, the yellow Turtle, the Salmon (already has blue), the complete Beaver, or the Owl under the Coyote, so it goes straight back to the bowl.

Rule it teaches: Turtle and Salmon scores rise steeply at the top; a stone you can't place returns to the bowl. Manual §7 Turtle and Salmon tables, §5, §9 The Coyote

Deer: which stone to give up?

Medium

You play Use Spirit Power & Recover Cards and choose the Deer: move 1 stone from your board back to the bowl, then take 2 stones from the bowl. Your Owl has a finished blue pair and two lonely halves.

BowlGW
Spirit Power on topDeerBear

Your question

Which stone do you send back to the bowl, and which two stones do you take?

  1. Remove a purple from the Turtle; green and white both finish Owl pairs
  2. Remove the lone green from the Owl; take green → Rabbit and white → Owl
  3. Remove the yellow from the Salmon; take green → Rabbit and white → Owl
  4. Remove the red from the Rabbit; take green and white for the Owl
Show the answer

B — net +10 (13 → 23). Removing the lone white instead and taking white → Rabbit, green → Owl is just as good.

ChoiceNet
A: Turtle 3 → 1, two Owl pairs +8−2 + 8 = +6
B: lone half-pair costs 0; Rabbit +6, Owl pair +40 + 10 = +10
C: Salmon 1 → 0; Rabbit +6, Owl pair +4−1 + 10 = +9
D: Rabbit was 0 anyway; two Owl pairs +8 (Rabbit now needs 2 more)0 + 8 = +8

A half-pair on the Owl scores nothing, so it is the cheapest stone to give up. The removed stone lands in the bowl and can even be taken straight back. Checked against every possible removal: the best Deer play is worth +10.

Rule it teaches: Owl only scores completed pairs (4 points each), so lone halves are free to move; the Deer must remove first, then take two. Manual §7 Owl, §8 Deer, §12 FAQ (Deer)

Spirit Power timing: Eagle or not?

Medium

Three of your cards are face-down, so you may use a Spirit Power. The two face-up Spirit Power cards are Eagle (take 1 stone, recover your cards, then take another turn) and Wolf (draw 4 stones into the bowl, take 1). Your opponent has a red Turtle too and wants those reds.

Your question

What is your best play, and how many points does it gain before your opponent moves again?

  1. Take Two: red → Turtle, green → Rabbit
  2. Spirit Power (Eagle): take a stone, recover all cards, then Take Two in your extra turn
  3. Spirit Power (Wolf): add 4 stones and take 1
  4. Take One: green → Rabbit
Show the answer

B — +15 points and all six cards face-up again.

StoneGain
Red → Turtle (4 → 5 stones: 9 → 14)+5
Red → Owl (finishes the red pair)+4
Green → Rabbit (blue, yellow, green)+6
Eagle, then Take Two: all three stones+15

A takes the two best stones (+5 +6 = +11) and leaves a red for your opponent. C and D take only one stone you know about (+6 at best); Wolf's four new stones are random and also feed your opponent. Eagle is two turns in a row: the first stone, then a full recovered hand to play Take Two on the stones that are still there.

Rule it teaches: Eagle: take 1, recover your cards, then take another turn immediately. Spirit Power needs 3 other cards face-down. Manual §6 Use Spirit Power & Recover Cards, §8 Eagle, §12 FAQ (Eagle)

Where does the Coyote go?

Hard

You play Add Three Stones & Move Coyote. The bowl was empty; you drew blue, a Spirit Stone and white. Spirit Stones out of the bag: 3, so the game goes on. Now you may put the Coyote on any animal on Ben's board. Ben still has Take Two face-up.

BowlBSW
Spirit Stones out of the bag3 / 5

Your question

Where should the Coyote go to keep Ben's next turn as small as possible?

  1. Turtle
  2. Salmon
  3. Owl
  4. Rabbit
Show the answer

A — Turtle. Ben's best Take Two reply for each Coyote spot (all options checked):

Coyote on…Ben gains
No Coyote: blue → Turtle (14), Spirit Stone → Turtle (28)+19
Coyote on Turtle: white → Salmon, Spirit Stone → Salmon (4 → 12); or blue → Salmon, Spirit Stone → Rabbit+8
Coyote on Salmon+19
Coyote on Owl (already closed: wasted)+19
Coyote on Rabbit or Beaver+19

The Coyote also blocks Spirit Stones, so it stops Ben from finishing and doubling his Turtle: you deny him 11 points on his very next turn. A section that already holds a Spirit Stone is closed anyway, so placing the Coyote there does nothing.

When it backfires: the Coyote stays on Ben's board, and in a tie the player with the Coyote on their board wins. Late in a close game, moving it onto your opponent hands them the tie-breaker (see scenario 7). Also notice you drew a Spirit Stone into the bowl: Add cards feed both players.

Rule it teaches: The Coyote blocks all stones, including Spirit Stones, on one opponent animal. Ties go to the player with the Coyote. Manual §9 The Coyote, §11 Tie-breaker, §12 FAQ (closed section)

The last Spirit Stone

Hard

Score: you 38, Ben 52. Ben put the Coyote on your Salmon a few turns ago. Four Spirit Stones were out (one on your Beaver, two on Ben's board, one in the bowl). You play Spirit Power: Bison — draw 3 stones into the bowl, take 1 and place it, then return 1 stone from the bowl to the bag. You drew a Spirit Stone, blue and purple, so 5 are now out.

BowlSRSBP
Spirit Stones out of the bag5 / 5

Your question

Which stone do you take, and which stone do you return to the bag?

  1. Spirit Stone → Turtle; return the red. Five stay out, the game ends now.
  2. Spirit Stone → Turtle; return the other Spirit Stone so the game goes on.
  3. Purple → Rabbit; return a Spirit Stone so the game goes on.
  4. Spirit Stone → Owl; return the red.
Show the answer

A — you win the tie. Your full green Turtle (14) with a Spirit Stone becomes 28: 38 + 14 = 52, equal to Ben's 52. Returning a regular stone leaves 5 Spirit Stones out at the end of your turn, so the game ends immediately. The totals are tied, and the Coyote is on your board, so you win.

ChoiceResult
A: Turtle 14 → 28, game ends 52–52, Coyote breaks the tieWin
B: 52–52, game continues; Ben takes purple → Turtle (9 → 14)Ben leads 57–52
C: Rabbit +6 (44); a Spirit Stone stays in the bowl, Ben puts it on his full Salmon (13 → 26)Ben leads 65–44
D: Owl 8 → 16 = 46, game endsLose 46–52

The end check happens at the end of the turn, after Bison's return step, so the Bison lets you choose whether the game ends. Your Salmon is under the Coyote, so it can't take the Spirit Stone; the Turtle is the biggest thing you can double (+14). Ben's quoted replies are his best single-stone takes.

Rule it teaches: The game ends when 5 Spirit Stones are out at the end of a turn; Bison can return one first. Ties go to the player with the Coyote. Manual §8 Bison, §10 End of the Game, §11 Tie-breaker
